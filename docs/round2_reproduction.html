Round 2: Faithfully Reproducing the Field SOTA — and Why moyu Still Wins

We qualified for the IJCAI-2026 Chinese-Standard-Mahjong final (Round 1: rank 11/16). For Round 2 (July 7) a new bot may be submitted. This is the honest record of trying to build one that beats our own champion moyu — by faithfully reproducing the published top-bot methods — and failing, credibly.

The honest starting point

Our earlier "RL is a dead end" verdict was based on an approximation, not the published recipes. Challenged on it ("have you actually reproduced Tjong?"), the honest answer was no. So we reproduced them properly. First, the base.

The base: SL from the official corpus (the pivot)

Distilling moyu into new architectures is imitation-ceiling-bound (an SE clone lost 0–1 of 80 games to moyu). The fix — the recipe the #1 field bots use — is supervised learning from scratch on the full official data. We built and verified a preprocessor that replays the 98,209-game official record through the deploy feature encoder: 5,126,868 (38×4×9) decisions, 100% legal, 0 errors (and found+fixed a real response2action Chi-encoding bug along the way).

From-scratch SE-ResNets reach ~0.90 next-action agreement — near Tjong's reported 94.6%. But agreement is not play:

The SL ceiling is not a capacity problem — every architecture, at every size, loses to moyu. Bigger is sometimes worse.

The faithful reproductions

Three published methods, implemented properly this time, each measured head-to-head vs moyu:

The sharpest single result. The final attempt distilled moyu directly, hoping for a moyu-strength base to apply the defense lever on. It reached 88.7% action-agreement with moyu — and still lost −19/game. You cannot distill your way to moyu: an 88.7%-agreeing student captures the easy discards but loses on the ~11% high-leverage decisions that decide games. moyu's strength is the full 40-block net + chunjiandu specialization + NET-PIMC search, not a copyable policy. Agreement ≠ play, at its starkest — and the explanation for the entire campaign.
The unifying result. Every mechanism genuinely works when reproduced faithfully — fan-backward raises fan-per-win, the defense head predicts deal-in at AUC 0.85, the rerank measurably helps. But each operates on a base policy weaker than moyu, and adjusts only at the margin (+4/game defense, ±2/game fan) — nowhere near moyu's 10–25/game base-strength lead. moyu's lineage (BC → PBT-champion → distill-chunjiandu → NET-PIMC) yields a stronger base than official-data SL plus any of these methods. The only route to a moyu-strength base via RL is full-net RL, which is compute-infeasible (~50 min/iteration) — the wall this project has hit from the start.

Why this is a credible null, not a lazy one

  • The data pipeline is verified (legality 100%, a real bug found), not assumed.
  • Every claim is a head-to-head gauntlet under a noise-floor-aware protocol (240–320 games, duplicate walls, thread-pinned bots, 0 illegal), not an agreement proxy — the project's iron lesson is agreement ≠ play, reconfirmed here (0.90 val nets lose by 10+/game).
  • The published mechanisms reproduce (fan rises, danger is predicted) — so the null is "these methods don't overcome moyu's base here," not "we implemented them wrong."

Decision for Round 2

moyu is the submission. It is the strongest bot we have, robust (0 illegal across thousands of games), and nothing built from the official data + the published methods beats it past the noise floor. The negative result is the contribution: a faithful, fully-logged demonstration that the field's published levers (fan-backward, transformer policy, defense head) are real and functional but insufficient to top a strong, search-augmented, specialization-distilled base — the Evaluation Gap thesis, made concrete.
